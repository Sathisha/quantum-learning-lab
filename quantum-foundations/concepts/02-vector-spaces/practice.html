Q11Answer (B)

Which of these is not a vector space over ℂ (with the usual addition and scalar multiplication)?

  • Aℂ³
  • Bthe set of unit vectors in ℂ²
  • Call 2×2 complex matrices
  • Dpolynomials of degree ≤ 2, complex coefficients
Solution

Test for a vector space: it must contain the zero vector, and adding two members or scaling a member must keep you inside the set.

Unit vectors: e1 = (1,0) and e2 = (0,1) both have length 1. e1 + e2 = (1,1) has length √2 ≠ 1 → not closed under addition ✗ 2·e1 has length 2 → not closed under scaling ✗ Zero vector has length 0 → not in the set ✗ (B) fails. (A), (C), (D) pass all tests.

Quantum link: qubit states are unit vectors, but we always do the maths inside the full space ℂ² and then normalise. A superposition |0⟩ + |1⟩ only becomes a valid state after dividing by √2.

Q12Answer (D) dependent over ℂ

Let u = (1, i)T and w = (i, −1)T. Which statement is true?

  • Au, w linearly independent over ℂ
  • Bu ⊥ w
  • Cw = −u
  • Du, w linearly dependent over ℂ
Solution
Try w = c·u. First entry: i = c·1 → c = i. Check second entry: c·i = i·i = −1 ✓ matches w's −1. So w = i·u: one is a complex multiple of the other. Linearly dependent over ℂ (D) (B) ⟨u,w⟩ = conj(1)·i + conj(i)·(−1) = i + (−i)(−1) = 2i ≠ 0 → not ⊥ (C) −u = (−1, −i) ≠ (i, −1)

Subtle point: over the real numbers ℝ you may only scale by real numbers, and no real c turns u into w. So over ℝ they would be independent. The field matters.

Quantum link: w = i·u means w is the same physical state as u, differing only by a global phase i. Global phases are invisible to measurement.

Q13Answer (C) 3, 6*

The dimension of ℂ³ as a vector space over ℂ, and over ℝ, is respectively

  • A3, 3
  • B6, 3
  • C3, 6
  • D6, 6
Solution
Over ℂ: basis {e1, e2, e3}. Any (a, b, c) = a·e1 + b·e2 + c·e3 with a,b,c ∈ ℂ. → 3 Over ℝ: coefficients must be real, so each complex entry needs two real numbers. Basis {e1, i·e1, e2, i·e2, e3, i·e3}. → 6 (dim over ℂ, dim over ℝ) = (3, 6)

*The pasted question dropped the ℂ/ℝ symbols, so the order is my reading. The facts are fixed: 3 over ℂ, 6 over ℝ. If your sheet lists ℝ first, the answer is (B).

Quantum link: a qubit lives in ℂ² (dimension 2 over ℂ). After removing normalisation and global phase, 2 real numbers remain, which is why a qubit fits on the surface of the Bloch sphere.
