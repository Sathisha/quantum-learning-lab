The AI/ML maths bridge

Slopes, feature vectors, a neural-network layer, gradient descent and softmax. Everything here reuses concepts 03–06: vectors, dot products, matrices and probabilities.

02Back to basics: slope and the derivative

Slope = rise ÷ run. Pick two points on a straight line; the slope is how much it goes up divided by how far it goes across. A positive slope climbs to the right, a negative slope falls to the right, zero slope is flat.

A curve has a different slope at every point. The derivative is the slope at one exact point: zoom in until the curve looks like a straight line, and measure that line's slope. Two rules are all you need today:

  • The slope of w² at any point is 2w.
  • The slope of (w − 3)² is 2(w − 3). It is the same bowl shifted 3 to the right, so the rule shifts too.

Tiny worked example. Slope of the line through (1, 3) and (3, 7), then the slope of w² at w = 3, checked with a tiny step:

rise = 7 − 3 = 4
run  = 3 − 1 = 2
slope = 4 ÷ 2 = 2

slope of w² at w = 3:  2 × 3 = 6
check with a tiny step from 3 to 3.01:
3.01² = 3.01 × 3.01 = 9.0601
3²    = 9
rise  = 9.0601 − 9 = 0.0601
run   = 3.01 − 3   = 0.01
slope ≈ 0.0601 ÷ 0.01 = 6.01   ✓ close to 6

03The idea

A machine-learning model sees every data point as a vector of features (a house might be (area, rooms, age)). A layer of a neural network is just the matrices concept's matrix-times-vector plus a bias: y = Wx + b. Training means finding the numbers in W that make a loss small, and the tool for that is the slope: step downhill, a little at a time. At the end, softmax turns raw scores into probabilities that add to 1, just like the probability concept.

04Diagrams

05Key formula: one gradient-descent step

w ← w − η · dL/dw

Read it aloud: "w becomes w minus eta times d L by d w."

New weight = old weight − (learning rate × slope of the loss here). The minus sign means: go against the slope, which is downhill.

06Worked example

Loss L(w) = (w − 1)², start at w = 5, learning rate η = 0.25. Take two steps (this is diagram C at its default setting).

Slope rule:  dL/dw = 2(w − 1)

Step 1, w = 5
dL/dw = 2 × (5 − 1)
      = 2 × 4
      = 8
η × slope = 0.25 × 8 = 2
new w = 5 − 2 = 3

Step 2, w = 3
dL/dw = 2 × (3 − 1)
      = 2 × 2
      = 4
η × slope = 0.25 × 4 = 1
new w = 3 − 1 = 2

Check: is the loss going down?
L(5) = (5 − 1)² = 4² = 16
L(3) = (3 − 1)² = 2² = 4
L(2) = (2 − 1)² = 1² = 1
16 → 4 → 1   ✓ falling towards the minimum at w = 1

07Common mistakes

  • Adding the gradient instead of subtracting it. w + η·slope walks uphill and the loss grows. The rule is always minus.
  • Losing a minus sign inside the slope. If w is left of the minimum, the slope is negative, and "minus a negative" becomes plus: w moves right. Write 0 − 0.1 × (−6) out in full before simplifying.
  • Softmax on the raw scores. Dividing 2, 1, 0 by their sum (3) is not softmax. Take eˣ of each score first, then divide by the total of those.

08Practice

Take one gradient-descent step on f(w) = (w − 3)² starting from w = 0 with learning rate η = 0.1. Bonus: compute softmax of the scores (2, 1, 0).

Show answer 
Slope rule:  df/dw = 2(w − 3)
df/dw at w = 0 = 2 × (0 − 3)
               = 2 × (−3)
               = −6
η × slope = 0.1 × (−6) = −0.6
new w = 0 − (−0.6)
      = 0 + 0.6
      = 0.6

Check:
f(0)   = (0 − 3)²   = (−3)²   = 9
f(0.6) = (0.6 − 3)² = (−2.4)² = 5.76
9 → 5.76   ✓ loss went down; w moved towards 3

Bonus: softmax(2, 1, 0)
e² ≈ 7.389
e¹ ≈ 2.718
e⁰ = 1
total = 7.389 + 2.718 + 1 = 11.107
p₁ = 7.389 ÷ 11.107 ≈ 0.665
p₂ = 2.718 ÷ 11.107 ≈ 0.245
p₃ = 1     ÷ 11.107 ≈ 0.090
Check: 0.665 + 0.245 + 0.090 = 1.000   ✓

09Check it in NumPy

import numpy as np
w, eta = 0.0, 0.1
grad = 2 * (w - 3)
print(grad, round(w - eta * grad, 4))                # -6.0 0.6
s = np.array([2, 1, 0]); print(np.exp(s) / np.exp(s).sum())  # [0.665 0.245 0.09]
